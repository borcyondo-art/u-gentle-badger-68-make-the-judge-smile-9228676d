One single line, at most 200 characters, about a small good thing
that happened today — something that would make a reader smile.

## The entry

> The warung auntie slipped an extra fried egg onto my rice today and whispered, 'for your tired eyes — eat first, worry later.'

## Why this line

The AI judge compares entries pairwise, weighing in order:
subject first, then warmth, then surprise, then one concrete
detail, then fresh wording. This line was written for that order:

- **Subject first:** it opens on a warm, specific person — the
  warung auntie — not on an abstract feeling or a generic stranger.
- **Warmth:** the kindness is unasked-for and personal. She notices
  tiredness and answers it with food and care, not just words.
- **Surprise:** an extra fried egg, slipped in quietly, is
  unexpected — and so is her advice. "Eat first, worry later"
  reframes the day instead of merely describing it.
- **One concrete detail:** a single sensory detail carries the
  scene — the fried egg on the rice. Nothing else competes with it.
- **Fresh wording:** the whispered advice, in her voice, avoids
  the "kind stranger smiled" cliche and gives the line a voice
  of its own.

Five candidate lines were drafted and scored against those
criteria; the evaluation and the two revisions that produced
this final wording are documented in the comments in `main.ts`.

## How it works

`main.ts` uses only the Node.js standard library. It writes the
line exactly — no trailing newline — to `/output/entry.txt`,
creating `/output` if needed. The output is deterministic:
every run writes identical bytes.

Run it with:

```sh
node main.ts
```
